# The Prodigal Son and the General Story of Jonah

|Element|The Prodigal Son (Luke 15:11-32)|Jonah|
|---|---|---|
|The offending/undeserving party|The younger son — squanders his inheritance "with riotous living" (Luke 15:13)|Nineveh — "their wickedness has come up before me" (Jonah 1:2)|
|Recognition of wrongdoing|"I have sinned against heaven, and in your sight" (Luke 15:18, 21)|The king's decree: "let them turn everyone from his evil way, and from the violence in his hands" (Jonah 3:8)|
|The father/God's response to repentance|"While he was still far off, his father saw him, and was moved with compassion, and ran" (Luke 15:20)|"God saw their works, that they turned from their evil way. God relented of the disaster" (Jonah 3:10)|
|Celebration/mercy extended|A robe, a ring, sandals, the fattened calf — "let us eat, and celebrate" (Luke 15:22-23)|Judgment averted entirely; the city is spared (Jonah 3:10)|
|The one who objects to the mercy shown|The elder brother — "he was angry, and would not go in" (Luke 15:28)|Jonah — "it displeased Jonah exceedingly, and he was angry" (Jonah 4:1)|
|Stated grounds for the objection|"These many years I have served you... you never gave me a goat... but when this your son came, who has devoured your living with prostitutes, you killed the fattened calf for him" (Luke 15:29-30)|"I knew that you are a gracious God, and merciful, slow to anger, and abundant in loving kindness, and you relent of doing harm" (Jonah 4:2)|
|Nature of the objection|Self-righteous comparison — emphasis on his own faithful service versus the other's unworthiness|Theological complaint — objection to God's character being exercised toward the unworthy|
|Father/God's response to the objector|"Son, you are always with me, and all that is mine is yours. But it was appropriate to celebrate and be glad, for this, your brother, was dead, and is alive again" (Luke 15:31-32)|"You have had respect for the vine... shouldn't I have respect for Nineveh, that great city, in which are more than one hundred twenty thousand persons... and also much cattle?" (Jonah 4:10-11)|
|Form of the closing appeal|A statement given directly to the elder son, grounding compassion in the fact of restored life ("was dead, and is alive again")|A question posed directly to Jonah, grounding compassion in the fact of created life/ignorance ("who can't discern between their right hand and their left hand")|
|Resolution recorded|Not recorded — the parable ends on the father's appeal, with no stated response from the elder brother|Not recorded — the book ends on YHWH's question, with no stated response from Jonah|

# Personal Observations

- Both accounts end on the same deliberately open note: a person who has legitimate standing with God (Jonah as covenant prophet; the elder son as the one who "never disobeyed," Luke 15:29) resents mercy shown to someone who, by their reckoning, hasn't earned it — and in both cases, the one extending mercy answers not by conceding the point but by reframing the entire situation around compassion for what was lost, restored, or spared. 
- Neither text records whether the objecting party actually comes inside or answers the question posed to them, leaving the resolution to the reader/hearer rather than the narrative itself.
- The elder brother and Jonah both invoke their own faithfulness/service as the implicit basis for their complaint, though neither the parable nor Jonah's own words state outright that this is unjust reasoning — both texts instead reframe the question toward the worth of the one shown mercy rather than directly refuting the complainer's self-assessment
