# Jonah
# Setting

## Characters and People Groups

- Named Characters: Jonah, son of Amittai; the king of Nineveh
- People Groups: Ninevites; sailors (unnamed, multi-national crew)

## Setting and Main Storyline

- **Setting**: The Mediterranean Sea (Joppa to Tarshish route), the belly of a great fish, and the city of Nineveh in Assyria.
- **Main Storyline**: YHWH commissions Jonah to proclaim judgment against Nineveh; Jonah flees by sea instead, is swallowed by a great fish after being cast overboard during a storm, prays from within it and is delivered, then obeys the renewed commission. Nineveh repents at his proclamation and YHWH relents from destroying it, provoking Jonah's anger, which YHWH addresses through a series of questions and an object lesson involving a plant
- **When**:
    - **Written**: Not stated in the text; traditionally associated with the prophet named in 2 Kings 14:25, placing composition at or after the events of the 8th century BCE
    - **Occurred**: During or near the reign of Jeroboam II of Israel (2 Kings 14:23-25)
- **Where**: Joppa, the Mediterranean Sea, Nineveh (Assyria)

## Repeated Words

- Great, gadol (גָּדוֹל), 14 occurrences (great city, great wind, great storm, great fish, great fear, great evil)
- God/Yahweh, elohim/YHWH (אֱלֹהִים / יְהוָה), used throughout as the central actor of every scene
- Evil, ra'ah (רָעָה), 9 occurrences (wickedness, evil way, disaster, Jonah's displeasure)
- Arise/rise, qum (קוּם), 7 occurrences (Jonah's repeated commissioning and his own risings and descents)
- Sea, yam (יָם), 8 occurrences
- Appoint/prepare, manah (מָנָה), 4 occurrences (fish, plant, worm, wind — below the 5-count threshold but structurally significant) 

## Connections

- Genesis 6-9 — the Flood narrative shares the pattern of widespread human wickedness "coming before" YHWH followed by a limited window before judgment (Genesis 6:13; Jonah 1:2)
- Genesis 18:20-21 — Sodom's "cry" reaching YHWH before investigation, structurally parallel to Nineveh's wickedness "coming up before" Him
- Exodus 34:6-7 — the covenant formula Jonah quotes directly as his complaint (Jonah 4:2)
- Matthew 12:39-41; 16:4; Luke 11:29-32 — Yeshua's direct application of Jonah's three days and Nineveh's repentance to His own resurrection and to "this generation"
- Jeremiah 18:7-8 — the general prophetic principle of relenting judgment upon repentance, matching Jonah 3:10 almost verbatim in structure
- Nahum, Zephaniah 2:13-15 — Nineveh's later, unaverted destruction, providing the historical sequel to the mercy shown in Jonah's account

# Footnotes

- **Blessings or curses found**: No formal covenant blessing/curse formula is pronounced; Nineveh's forty-day pronouncement functions as a conditional curse (destruction) averted by repentance (Jonah 3:4, 10)
- **Covenants made**: No covenant is formally established in the book; Jonah's prayer (2:9) references vow-fulfillment ("I will pay that which I have vowed") rather than covenant-making
- **Idioms the original audience would have known**:
    - "Arise and go" (qum lekh) — a recognized prophetic commissioning idiom, not unique to Jonah (cf. Genesis 22:2; Jonah 1:2, 3:2)
    - "Three days and three nights" — an idiom for a complete, whole period of time rather than a strict 72-hour count, drawn on by Yeshua in Matthew 12:40
    - "From the presence of Yahweh" — understood culturally not as claiming YHWH's omnipresence could be escaped, but as fleeing the place/context of prophetic service and accountability
- **Appointed Times mentioned or alluded to**: None of the formal appointed times (moedim) are referenced; the closest structural parallel is the forty-day period itself (Jonah 3:4), echoing other biblical forty-day/year spans of testing (Genesis 7:4; Numbers 14:33-34) rather than a fixed appointed time on Israel's calendar

## Overview

The book of Jonah exhibits a two-panel symmetry often noted in its narrative arrangement — chapters 1-2 and chapters 3-4 mirror each other in sequence, while a chiasm can also be traced across the whole book centered on Jonah's prayer/deliverance. Below is one way of laying out the structure directly from the text's own content and sequence.

## A — Commission and Flight (Jonah 1:1-3)

- YHWH's word comes to Jonah: "Arise, go to Nineveh, that great city, and proclaim against it" (Jonah 1:2, WEB)
- Jonah flees instead toward Tarshish, "from the presence of Yahweh" (1:3)

## ---- B — Gentile Sailors Fear and Turn to YHWH (Jonah 1:4-16)

- A storm threatens death; pagan sailors each cry to their own god (1:4-5)
- Jonah is identified by lot as the cause (1:7)
- Jonah confesses YHWH as maker of "the sea and the dry land" (1:9)
- Jonah is cast into the sea at his own instruction (1:12-15)
- The sailors "feared Yahweh exceedingly," sacrificed, and made vows (1:16)

## ---- ---- C — Jonah's Prayer from the Depths / Deliverance (Jonah 1:17-2:10)

- "Yahweh prepared a great fish to swallow up Jonah" (1:17)
- Jonah prays from "the belly of Sheol" (2:2), recounting his descent into the waters, weeds, and the pit (2:3-6)
- Declaration: "salvation belongs to Yahweh" (2:9)
- YHWH speaks to the fish, and it vomits Jonah onto dry land (2:10)

## ---- ---- C' — Renewed Commission and Nineveh's Repentance (Jonah 3:1-10)

- YHWH's word comes to Jonah a second time: "Arise, go to Nineveh... preach to it the message that I give you" (3:1-2) — directly echoing 1:1-2
- Jonah proclaims: "Yet forty days, and Nineveh will be overthrown" (3:4)
- Nineveh, from the king to the least, repents in fasting and sackcloth (3:5-9)
- "God saw their works... God relented of the disaster" (3:10)

## ---- B' — Gentile City Spared, Jonah's Reaction (Jonah 4:1-4)

- Where the Gentile sailors in B feared and turned to YHWH with joy, here Jonah — the Hebrew prophet — reacts to the same kind of divine mercy with anger: "it displeased Jonah exceedingly, and he was angry" (4:1)
- Jonah confesses YHWH's character directly, quoting the Exodus 34:6 formula (4:2), then asks to die (4:3)
- YHWH's first question to Jonah: "Is it right for you to be angry?" (4:4)

## A' — Object Lesson and Unanswered Question (Jonah 4:5-11)

- Jonah sits east of the city, still watching to see what will become of it (4:5) — a mirrored, passive echo of his earlier active flight from the commission in A
- YHWH appoints a plant, then a worm, then a scorching wind — Jonah's comfort given and removed, producing anger a second time (4:6-8)
- YHWH's closing rebuke reframes the entire book's tension: concern over a plant Jonah "didn't labor for" versus concern for "more than one hundred twenty thousand persons" in Nineveh (4:9-11)
- The book ends on YHWH's unanswered question — no recorded reply from Jonah

## Center of the Chiasm

- On this reading, the structural and theological center falls at C/C' — Jonah's own deliverance from death (2:1-10) is directly mirrored by Nineveh's own deliverance from threatened destruction (3:1-10). Both center on the same principle stated in Jonah's own mouth: YHWH is one "who relents of doing harm" (4:2) — demonstrated first in Jonah's own rescue, then in Nineveh's

## Observations Supporting This Structure

- The commissioning formula in 1:1-2 and 3:1-2 is nearly verbatim repeated, marking a clear structural seam the text itself creates
- Both "panels" (1:1-2:10 and 3:1-4:11) move from a divine word, through a Gentile group's response of fear/turning, to a resolution involving YHWH relenting or delivering
- The book's refusal to resolve Jonah's anger (ending on a question rather than Jonah's answer) breaks the symmetry deliberately — [Inference] this asymmetric, open ending may be the text's own way of turning the chiasm outward toward the reader, who is left to answer YHWH's question in Jonah's place, though this is not something the text states as its purpose
