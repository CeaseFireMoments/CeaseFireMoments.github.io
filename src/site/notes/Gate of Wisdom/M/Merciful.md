# Define

- Hebrew: rachum (רַחוּם) — "merciful, compassionate"; from the root r-ch-m, related to rechem (רֶחֶם), "womb" — in Hebrew mindset, this root carries the image of a deep, instinctive, almost parental compassion, the kind of visceral pity a mother has for her own child. When applied to YHWH, it describes compassion that responds to weakness, distress, or need, not favor granted apart from any such condition
- Greek: eleemon (ἐλεήμων) — "merciful"; the standard LXX rendering of rachum, later carried into NT vocabulary (e.g., "blessed are the merciful," eleemones, Matthew 5:7)

# Related Words

- Chanun (חַנּוּן) — "gracious," paired directly with rachum in the same verse and in the Exodus 34:6 formula it draws from
- Racham (רָחַם) — the verb form, "to have compassion, show [[Gate of Wisdom/Consequence/Disobedience/Mercy\|Mercy]]" (Psalm 103:13)
- Rechem (רֶחֶם) — "womb," the root noun from which rachum derives
- Chus (חוּס) — "to pity, spare," a related but distinct term used later in the same chapter (Jonah 4:10-11)
- Chesed (חֶסֶד) — "loving kindness, covenant faithfulness," used earlier in Jonah's own prayer (2:8), conceptually adjacent but carrying stronger covenant-loyalty overtones than rachum's more visceral compassion

# Additional Information

- Jonah 4:2 directly quotes the Sinai self-revelation formula: "Yahweh, Yahweh, a merciful (rachum) and gracious (chanun) God, slow to anger, and abundant in loving kindness and truth" (Exodus 34:6, WEB) — Jonah cites this as the reason he fled in the first place, before ever reaching Nineveh
	- This is the only occurrence of rachum in the book of Jonah, appearing in Jonah's own complaint rather than in praise or worship — the only instance in Scripture where this formula is quoted with resentment rather than as an act of praise or intercession
- The womb-image behind rachum appears elsewhere applied directly to YHWH's compassion: "as a father has compassion (racham) on his children, so Yahweh has compassion (racham) on those who fear him" (Psalm 103:13, WEB); "can a woman forget her nursing child... yet I will not forget you" (Isaiah 49:15, WEB)

# Connections

- Exodus 34:6-7 — the source formula
- Psalm 86:15; 103:8; 145:8 — worship contexts repeating the same formula
- Joel 2:13 — the same formula cited as grounds for national repentance, structurally parallel to Nineveh's own repentance in Jonah 3
- Matthew 5:7 — "blessed are the merciful (eleemones), for they shall obtain mercy (eleethesontai)" — NT continuation of the same root concept via the Greek eleos/eleemon
