# Define

- Greek: plesion (πλησίον) — "the one near/close by," used as a substantive meaning "neighbor"; in this saying it directly echoes and quotes Leviticus 19:18, carrying the full weight of the Torah command into the Greek text.
- Hebrew: rea (רֵעַ) — "neighbor, companion, fellow, associate"; in the Hebrew mindset this term does not require kinship or even nationality — it denotes anyone one is in relationship or proximity with, extending to the [[Gate of Wisdom/Beings/Stranger\|Stranger]] dwelling among Israel (Leviticus 19:34 uses the same command applied to the ger, גֵּר,.

# Related Words

- Ger (גֵּר) — "sojourner/[[Gate of Wisdom/Beings/Stranger\|Stranger]]," to whom the same love-command is explicitly extended in Leviticus 19:34
- Ahav (אָהַב) / agapao (ἀγαπάω) — "to love," the verb paired with rea/plesion in both the Hebrew and Greek forms of the command

# Additional Information

- Matthew 22:39 — "A second likewise is this, 'You shall love your neighbor as yourself.'" Yeshua directly quotes Leviticus 19:18.
- Leviticus 19:18 — the source text: "You shall not take vengeance, nor bear any grudge against the children of your people; but you shall love your neighbor as yourself. I am YHWH."
- Mark 12:31 and Luke 10:27 record the same command paired with loving God, forming the two greatest commandments.
- Luke 10:29-37 — when asked "who is my neighbor," Yeshua answers with the parable of the Good Samaritan, expanding rea/plesion beyond ethnic or religious boundary lines, consistent with Leviticus 19:34's inclusion of the ger.

# Connections

- Leviticus 19:18 — the Torah source of the command
- Leviticus 19:34 — the same love-command extended explicitly to the stranger (ger)
- Mark 12:31, Luke 10:27 — parallel NT citations of the same command
- Luke 10:29-37 — the Good Samaritan parable defining the scope of "neighbor"
