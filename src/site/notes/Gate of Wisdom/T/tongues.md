# Define

- Greek: glōssa (γλῶσσα) — the physical tongue as an organ of speech, and by extension a language or dialect distinct from another people's speech
- Hebrew: lashon (לָשׁוֹן) — tongue as speech-organ, and by extension a nation's or people's distinct language (cf. Genesis 10:5, Genesis 10:20, Genesis 10:31, "every one after his tongue")

# Related Words

- dialektos (διάλεκτος) — Acts 2:6, Acts 2:8 — "language/dialect," used alongside glōssa in the same passage to describe the languages heard by the crowd
- pneuma (πνεῦμα) — Acts 2:4 — "Spirit/'[[Gate of Wisdom/Ruach/Ruach\|Ruach]]," the agent giving the utterance
- apophthengomai (ἀποφθέγγομαι) — Acts 2:4 — "to speak out, declare," the verb describing how they spoke

# Additional Information

- In Acts 2:4, glōssa is qualified as heterais glōssais ("other tongues") — languages other than the speakers' own native tongue
- Acts 2:6-8 clarifies that this was intelligible human language: each person heard the disciples speaking in their own native dialektos
- Acts 2:9-11 lists the specific nations/languages represented, confirming glōssai here = actual known human languages, not unintelligible utterance

# Connections

- Genesis 11:1-9 — Babel, where one lashon (tongue) was confused into many; Some see Acts 2 as a reversal picture, where many tongues are now understood as one message. This is a debated topic.
- Acts 10:46, Acts 19:6 — later occurrences of speaking in tongues (glōssais) among Gentile and Ephesian believers
- 1 Corinthians 12:10, 1 Corinthians 12:28, 1 Corinthians 14:1-40 — Paul's extended teaching on glōssai as a spiritual gift, including interpretation
