- The term "Christian"
	- within the biblical text, the community of followers of Jesus called themselves disciples and belonged to "[[Gate of Wisdom/Beings/Human Beings/The Way\|The Way]]" 
	- they were called Christians by others — the text never shows them adopting the term as self-identification
	- "Christian" as a self-chosen identity marker is a post-biblical development (clearly attested with Ignatius, early 2nd century), not something Scripture itself records the disciples doing
	- Christian appears 3 times. The Way appears 6 times.

# Define

- Greek: Christianos (Χριστιανός) — "follower of Christ/Messiah," formed from Christos (Χριστός, "Anointed One") plus the Latin-style suffix -ianos denoting adherents/partisans of a person. First applied to the disciples at Antioch, indicating outsiders recognized them as a distinct group identified with the Messiah.
- Hebrew: Meshichi (מְשִׁיחִי) — "of/belonging to Mashiach (מָשִׁיחַ)," the Anointed One; conceptually parallel to Christianos, tying the label back to the Hebrew concept of anointing (kings, priests, prophets set apart for YHWH's service).

# Related Words

- Christos (Χριστός) — "Anointed One," Greek rendering of Mashiach (מָשִׁיחַ)
- Mashiach (מָשִׁיחַ) [[Gate of Wisdom/Beings/Heavenly Beings/Messiah\|Messiah]] — "Anointed One," root of the title applied to Yeshua
- Talmid (תַּלְמִיד) — "[[Gate of Wisdom/Beings/Human Beings/Disciple\|Disciple]]," the term used for followers before "Christian" arose as an **outsider** label. "Disciples" is the term used most consistently throughout the Gospels and Acts, both by others and among themselves
- [[Gate of Wisdom/Beings/Human Beings/The Way\|The Way]] the earliest and most consistent self-reference by the disciples themselves

# Additional Information

- Acts 11:26 states the disciples were first called Christians at Antioch, following Barnabas and Saul's year of teaching there.
- The term appears only three times in Scripture: Acts 11:26, Acts 26:28, and 1 Peter 4:16.
- In 1 Peter 4:16, bearing the name "Christian" is tied to suffering for the name rather than shame — "let him glorify God in this name."
# Connections

- Acts 26:28 — Agrippa's response to Paul, "with a little persuasion you would make me a Christian"
- 1 Peter 4:16 — suffering as a Christian is not shameful but a cause to glorify God
- Acts 11:19-26 — the broader context of the Antioch congregation where the name originated

# Extra Biblical Sources

Roman sources from the same era show the name was used by outsiders with hostility:
- Tacitus (Annals 15.44, c. 116 AD) says the populace called them "Christians," a name Tacitus treats as originating with "Christus," executed under Pilate — used in a context of blaming them for the Great Fire of Rome.
- Pliny the Younger's letter to Trajan (c. 111–113 AD) records that merely bearing "the name" (nomen ipsum) was treated as a capital offense, and asks Trajan for guidance on prosecuting "Christians" as such.
- Suetonius (Life of Claudius 25) mentions disturbances involving "Chrestus," often taken as an early garbled reference to Christ-followers.

Later apologists **Justin Martyr** (_First Apology_ 4) and **Tertullian** (_Apology_ 1–3) explicitly argue that Christians were condemned for the _name itself_, regardless of actual conduct — implying the label had become a legal liability by the mid-to-late 2nd century

**Ignatius of Antioch** (early 2nd century, martyred c. 107–117 AD), writing en route to his execution in Rome. Ignatius embraces the term fully and even coins "Christianity" (Christianismos) as a positive identity, contrasting it with Judaism in his letters (e.g., _To the Magnesians_ 10, _To the Romans_ 3). He shows no reluctance — by his generation the name is worn as a badge of honor, even unto death.
