- **Mother**: Not named in Scripture
- **Sibling(s)**: Not named in Scripture
- **Hebrew Meaning of Name**: Yonah (יוֹנָה) — "dove"
- **Other Relatives of Note**: None named

- **Other Descendants of Note**: None named

# Land and Dwelling

## Dwelling Places

- Gath Hepher, a town in Zebulun (2 Kings 14:25; Joshua 19:10-13)

## Journeys

- Commanded by YHWH to go to [[Gate of Wisdom/Dwelling Places/Realm of Humans/Nineveh\|Nineveh]] (Jonah 1:1-2)
- Fled instead toward Tarshish, boarding a ship at Joppa (Jonah 1:3)
	- Jonah flees to Tarshish just as all biblical characters attempt some kind of “return to Eden” by their own power and scheming.
	- Other resistant prophets in the Hebrew Bible, like Moses, Elijah, and Jeremiah
- Cast into the sea during the storm and swallowed by a great fish (Jonah 1:15-17)
- Vomited onto dry land after three days and three nights in the fish (Jonah 2:10)
- Went to Nineveh after a second call from YHWH (Jonah 3:1-4)
- Sat east of Nineveh under a booth to watch the city (Jonah 4:5)

# Occupation and Military

- Identified as a prophet of YHWH (2 Kings 14:25; Jonah 1:1)
- Prophesied and saw fulfilled the restoration of Israel's border from Lebo-hamath to the Sea of the Arabah, during Jeroboam II's reign (2 Kings 14:25)

# Medical and Death

- Three days and three nights in the belly of the fish, described as a near-death experience — "out of the belly of Sheol I cried" (Jonah 2:1-2, WEB)
- No death or burial account given in Scripture
- Referenced by Yeshua as a sign — "as Jonah was three days and three nights in the belly of the huge fish, so will the Son of Man be three days and three nights in the heart of the earth" (Matthew 12:39-40, WEB)

# The Ninevites

## Differences

- **Covenant standing**: 
	- Jonah is explicitly "a Hebrew" who "fear Yahweh, the God of heaven" (Jonah 1:9, WEB) — an insider to YHWH's covenant with Israel; 
	- the Ninevites are [[Gate of Wisdom/Beings/Human Beings/Gentile\|Gentile]]s with no prior covenant relationship to YHWH, addressed only because their "wickedness has come up before" Him (Jonah 1:2)
- **Response to YHWH's word**: 
	- Jonah receives a direct, personal commission and flees from it (Jonah 1:3); 
	- the Ninevites receive the message secondhand through a foreign prophet's brief proclamation and immediately believe and repent — "the people of Nineveh believed God" (Jonah 3:5, WEB)
- **Speed and completeness of repentance**: 
	- Nineveh's repentance is described as immediate and total — king, nobles, and commoners alike, even extending to animals in sackcloth (Jonah 3:5-8); 
	- Jonah's own repentance, by contrast, is not clearly resolved at all — the book ends with him still arguing with YHWH (Jonah 4:9-11)
- **Knowledge attributed to each**: 
	- YHWH describes Nineveh's population as those "who can't discern between their right hand and their left hand" (Jonah 4:11, WEB) — a description of spiritual/moral ignorance; 
	- Jonah, by contrast, possesses direct knowledge of YHWH's character, stating "I knew that you are a gracious God" (Jonah 4:2, WEB) — his resistance comes from knowledge, not ignorance
- **Object of anger/fear**: 
	- The Ninevites feared for their own survival and turned it into repentance ("who can tell if God will turn and relent... that we not perish?" Jonah 3:9, WEB); 
	- Jonah's anger is directed at YHWH's mercy itself, wishing Nineveh had been destroyed (Jonah 4:1-3)

## Commonalities

- **Both stand under the same divine evaluation of "evil" (ra'ah)**: 
	- Nineveh's "wickedness" (ra'ah, Jonah 1:2) and "evil way" (ra'ah, 3:10) is named directly; 
	- Jonah's own displeasure is described using the identical root — "it displeased Jonah exceedingly" is literally "it was evil (ra'ah), a great evil, to Jonah" (Jonah 4:1) — the text applies the same moral-evaluation vocabulary to both parties
- **Both are objects of YHWH's compassion despite fault**: 
	- YHWH relents from destroying Nineveh (Jonah 3:10) and also patiently reasons with Jonah rather than punishing his anger, rebuke, or death-wish (Jonah 4:4, 9-11) — both receive mercy rather than the response their conduct might warrant
- **Both respond to a word from YHWH with an initial posture that must be overcome**: 
	- Nineveh must overcome unbelief/indifference to repent; 
	- Jonah must overcome active resistance to obey — 
	- both narratives involve a turning, though Nineveh's turning is completed in the text and Jonah's is left open
- **Both are engaged directly by YHWH through pointed, personal address**: 
	- YHWH sends a specific messenger to Nineveh (Jonah 3:1-4) and personally reasons with Jonah through direct questions — "is it right for you to be angry?" (4:4), "is it right for you to be angry about the vine?" (4:9), and the closing appeal about the "more than 120,000 persons" (4:11) 
	- YHWH pursues both the pagan city and the disobedient prophet with comparable directness

## The Book's Structural Point

- The parallel and contrast together form the book's central irony: the Gentile city that "can't discern between their right hand and their left hand" (4:11) repents fully and immediately at a bare five-word Hebrew proclamation, while the prophet who possesses full knowledge of YHWH's revealed character (4:2, quoting Exodus 34:6) resists that same character to the end of the book — the ignorant respond rightly, and the informed resist

# Righteousness

- Delivered YHWH's message of judgment to Nineveh after his call, resulting in citywide repentance from the king to the least (Jonah 3:4-9)
- Prayed to YHWH from within the fish, acknowledging YHWH's deliverance and vowing sacrifice with thanksgiving (Jonah 2:1-9)
- Yeshua points to the Ninevites' repentance "at the proclamation of Jonah" as a standard by which "this generation" would be judged (Matthew 12:41; Luke 11:32)

# Unrighteousness

- Fled from the presence of YHWH rather than go to Nineveh (Jonah 1:3)
- Told the sailors to throw him into the sea rather than repent or petition YHWH himself (Jonah 1:12)
- Became angry when YHWH relented from destroying Nineveh, saying it would have been better to die (Jonah 4:1-3)
	- displeasure is stated directly, but what triggers it is not Nineveh's wickedness itself, but YHWH's mercy toward them after their repentance (Jonah 3:10-4:1). 
	- his objection was not primarily about Nineveh's character but about YHWH's character — he anticipated that if he preached, Nineveh might repent, and if they repented, YHWH would relent, and that outcome is precisely what he wanted to prevent. 
	- But he relied on this very same relenting for his running away.
	- Jonah 4:2's list of YHWH's attributes ("gracious, merciful, slow to anger, abundant in loving kindness") directly quotes the formula first given at Sinai: "Yahweh, Yahweh, a merciful and gracious God, slow to anger, and abundant in loving kindness and truth" (Exodus 34:6, WEB) — Jonah wields YHWH's own self-revelation as the basis for his complaint
- Complained a second time to the point of death when the plant that shaded him withered (Jonah 4:8-9)
- Displayed more concern for his own comfort (the plant) than for the "more than 120,000 persons" of Nineveh (Jonah 4:10-11)

# Other

- His confession names YHWH as "the God of heaven, who has made the sea and the dry land" (Jonah 1:9, WEB)
- The sign of Jonah is applied by Yeshua both to His resurrection (Matthew 12:39-40) and to His generation's need for repentance (Luke 11:29-30)
- YHWH's closing question to Jonah — concern over a plant versus concern over Nineveh's people and animals — ends the book unanswered (Jonah 4:10-11)
- Jonah's prior prophetic role, as recorded in 2 Kings 14:25, was one of pronouncing good news for Israel — territorial restoration and national expansion under Jeroboam II.
	- Jeroboam really bad guy. Jeroboam I caused Israel to split, and then put golden calves in the temple. 
	- Jonah prophesied favorable for him. So later, he would be known as the prophet who prophesied favorably for a terrible king. 

# Contemporaries
- Jonah's main contemporary prophets were Amos and Hosea, who also ministered to the northern kingdom of Israel during the 8th century BC.
	- Amos: Preached against social injustice and materialism in Israel during the same era of material prosperity
	- Hosea: Ministered around the same time, focusing on God's faithful love despite Israel's spiritual unfaithfulness.
- [[Zunpublished/Elijah\|Elijah]] and Elisha: Served as Jonah's immediate predecessors just before his time.
- [[Gate of Wisdom/Beings/Human Beings/Isaiah\|Isaiah]]: Began his prophetic ministry in the southern kingdom shortly after Jonah, Amos, and Hosea

## Overlapping Messaging: Jonah, Amos, Hosea

### Shared Historical Setting

- All three are dated to the 8th century BCE, during or near the reign of Jeroboam II of Israel (2 Kings 14:23-25; Hosea 1:1; Amos 1:1)
- Jonah is noted as active "in the days of Jeroboam the son of Joash king of Israel" (2 Kings 14:25, WEB) — the same king named in Hosea 1:1 and Amos 1:1

### YHWH's Sovereignty Over Gentile Nations

- Jonah: YHWH sends a prophet to Nineveh, a [[Gate of Wisdom/Beings/Human Beings/Gentile\|Gentile]] city, and it repents at his word (Jonah 3:5-10)
- Amos: opens with judgment oracles against surrounding Gentile nations — Damascus, Gaza, Tyre, Edom, Ammon, Moab — before turning to Judah and Israel (Amos 1:3-2:5)
- Hosea: less direct on Gentile nations, but affirms YHWH's universal claim — "Yahweh has a controversy with Israel" while His authority as Creator and Judge is assumed throughout (Hosea 4:1; 12:9)
- <mark style="background: #CD04BBA6;">Inference</mark> Read together, all three assume YHWH's rule is not confined to Israel — He judges and shows mercy to nations outside the covenant as well as within it

### Divine Compassion Amid Deserved Judgment

- Jonah: YHWH relents from destroying Nineveh once they repent, prompting Jonah's complaint that he knew YHWH to be "a gracious God, and merciful, slow to anger, and abundant in loving kindness" (Jonah 4:2, WEB)
- Hosea: YHWH's compassion overrides deserved wrath toward Israel — "How can I give you up, Ephraim?... my heart is turned within me, my compassion is aroused" (Hosea 11:8, WEB)
- Amos: compassion is less explicit but implied in the prophet's own intercession — "Lord Yahweh, forgive, I beg you! How could [[Gate of Wisdom/Beings/Human Beings/Jacob\|Jacob]] stand? For he is small" (Amos 7:2, WEB), and YHWH relents twice at Amos's plea (Amos 7:1-6)

### Judgment for National Sin

- Amos: condemns Israel for social injustice, oppression of the poor, and corrupt worship — "they sell the righteous for silver, and the needy for a pair of shoes" (Amos 2:6, WEB)
- Hosea: condemns [[Gate of Wisdom/Beings/Human Beings/House of Israel\|House of Israel]] for covenant unfaithfulness, described through the marriage metaphor of adultery/harlotry, alongside lack of knowledge of YHWH — "my people are destroyed for lack of knowledge" (Hosea 4:6, WEB)
- Jonah: judgment is pronounced against Nineveh for its wickedness, though the specific sins are not detailed as they are for Israel — "their wickedness has come up before me" (Jonah 1:2, WEB)

### The Call to Repentance and Return

- Hosea: repeatedly calls Israel to "return to Yahweh your God" (Hosea 14:1-2, WEB)
- Jonah: Nineveh's response models national repentance — fasting, sackcloth, and turning "from his evil way" at a king's decree (Jonah 3:5-9)
- Amos: repentance is called for indirectly through the refrain "yet you have not returned to me" following each act of discipline (Amos 4:6-11), and directly — "seek me, and you will live" (Amos 5:4, WEB)

### Reluctant or Resistant Response

- Amos describes his own reluctant call to prophesy despite being "a herdsman, and a farmer of sycamore figs" — "Yahweh took me from following the flock, and Yahweh said to me, 'Go, prophesy to my people Israel'" (Amos 7:14-15, WEB)
	- It’s crucially important to note that Jeroboam’s border restoration and Jonah’s prophecy are intentionally reversed by an oracle from the prophet Amos. So whatever positive associations we had in 2 Kings 14 must be tempered by the eventual reversal of Jonah’s prophecy brought on by Israel’s apostasy.

# Personal Observations
- Jonah's attitude toward the Ninivites sound very similar to the attitude of the Leaders of Judaism at the time of Jesus toward those outside Judaism. 
- Jonah's own resistance to YHWH's mercy toward Nineveh (Jonah 4:1-3) forms a striking contrast to Hosea and Amos, both of whom plead for or model compassion toward the guilty (Hosea 11:8; Amos 7:2-3) — the prophet in Jonah becomes a foil rather than a model
