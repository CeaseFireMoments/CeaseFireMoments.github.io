# Birth and Ancestors

- **Timeframe**: Reigned during the 8th century BC
	- contemporary of the prophets Jonah, Amos and Hosea (2 Kings 14:23; Amos 1:1; Hosea 1:1)
- **Place**: Northern Kingdom of Israel
	- [[Gate of Wisdom/Beings/Human Beings/House of Israel\|House of Israel]]
- **Father**: Joash (Jehoash) king of Israel (2 Kings 14:23)
- **Mother**: Not named in Scripture
- **Sibling(s)**: Not named in Scripture
- **Hebrew Meaning of Name**: Jeroboam — Yarov'am (יָרָבְעָם) — "the people are many" or "he who contends for the people" or "he who increases the people"
- **Other Relatives of Note**:
    - Zechariah — son, succeeded him on the throne (2 Kings 14:29; 15:8)
    - Jehu — great-great-grandfather, founder of the dynasty (2 Kings 9:2; 14:23)
    - Jehoahaz — grandfather (2 Kings 13:1; 14:1)

# Land and Dwelling

## Dwelling Places

- Samaria — capital of the Northern Kingdom, where he reigned (2 Kings 14:23)

## Journeys

- Restored the territory of Israel from the entrance of Hamath to the Sea of the Arabah (2 Kings 14:25, 28)
- Recovered Damascus and Hamath — formerly belonging to Judah — for Israel (2 Kings 14:28)

# Occupation and Military

- King of Israel — reigned 41 years in Samaria (2 Kings 14:23)
- Highly successful military commander who expanded Israel's borders to their greatest extent since Solomon
- Restored border from Lebo-hamath in the north to the Sea of the Arabah in the south (2 Kings 14:25)
- Recovered Damascus and Hamath (2 Kings 14:28)
- All accomplished in fulfillment of the word of YHWH through [[Gate of Wisdom/Beings/Human Beings/Jonah\|Jonah]] ben Amittai (2 Kings 14:25)
- For the average citizen and the lower class, they viewed the era as a time of cruel systemic injustice.
- The prophets of the era—most notably Amos and Hosea—viewed Jeroboam II's reign with utter dread, condemning it as an age of moral degeneracy and spiritual bankruptcy.
	- In Amos 6:13-14, Amos confronts the nation for gloating over territorial victories. Amos specifically declares that God will raise up a nation (Assyria) to crush Israel from Lebo-hamath to the valley of the Arabah (the Dead Sea area).
	- Hosea is screaming at God's chosen people to repent. Hosea explicitly warns that because Israel refuses to repent, they will be conquered and exiled by Assyria (Hosea 9:3; 11:5)
	- Jonah is called to Nineveh (the capital of Assyria) - the very empire Amos and Hosea are saying will crush Israel.
- The king successfully seized vital trade routes and extracted heavy tribute from conquered territories like Damascus. This brought an influx of luxury goods—such as ivory, fine wine, and rare spices—into the capital. The wealthy elite, aristocrats, and merchants viewed the restoration of Israel's borders as a sign of permanent security and divine favor.
- Unlike other prophets who confronted the king's idolatry, Jonah served as a bearer of good news for Jeroboam’s administration. He delivered a prophecy predicting that Jeroboam II would successfully expand Israel's borders and reclaim lost territory. History shows this came true, bringing massive wealth to the upper classes.
	- The text explicitly states that Jeroboam II "did evil in the eyes of the Lord". Yet, Jonah still willingly delivered a message of victory to him. This points to Jonah's specific theological view.
	- Amos essentially took Jonah's prophecy and reversed it, warning the people that the very land Jonah promised they would win would soon be stripped away due to their corruption.
	- Hosea undid the false sense of divine endorsement that Jonah’s military prophecy helped create.

# Medical and Death

# Righteousness

- YHWH used him as the instrument to save Israel, seeing their affliction — "there was none to help Israel" (2 Kings 14:26–27)
- His military successes were prophetically foretold by Jonah ben Amittai of Gath-hepher (2 Kings 14:25) — YHWH fulfilled the word through him despite his spiritual failures
- YHWH did not blot out Israel's name under his watch (2 Kings 14:27)

# Unrighteousness

- Followed in the sin of Jeroboam ben Nebat — perpetuating the golden calves at Bethel and Dan (2 Kings 14:24; 1 Kings 12:28–30)
- "He did that which was evil in the sight of YHWH" — the standard indictment of the northern kings (2 Kings 14:24)
- The prophets Amos and Hosea ministered during his reign, both delivering sharp rebukes of the social and spiritual corruption of Israel at this time
    - Amos condemned the wealthy for oppressing the poor, selling the righteous for silver (Amos 2:6; 8:4–6)
    - Amos pronounced judgment directly tied to the house of Jeroboam (Amos 7:9)
    - Hosea condemned widespread harlotry, idolatry, and covenant breaking (Hosea 4:1–2; 8:4–6)
- The priest Amaziah of Bethel reported Amos to Jeroboam, framing Amos's prophecy as conspiracy against him (Amos 7:10–11)

# Other

- Jeroboam II is one of the most militarily and economically successful kings of the north, yet receives minimal treatment in Kings — a single paragraph (2 Kings 14:23–29)
- The prosperity of his reign masked deep covenant unfaithfulness — wealth and territorial expansion coexisted with injustice and idolatry
- He is not to be confused with Jeroboam ben Nebat (Jeroboam I), the first king of the Northern Kingdom who originated the sin of the calves (1 Kings 12)
- The phrase "he restored the border of Israel" (2 Kings 14:25) echoes the language of the Davidic era — yet unlike David, no covenant faithfulness accompanied it

# Extra Biblical Information

## Landmarks

- Samaria — capital city, modern Sebastos/Sebastiye in the northern West Bank
- Hamath — modern Hama, Syria; northern boundary of restored territory
- Gath-hepher — home of the prophet Jonah, modern Khirbet ez-Zurra near Nazareth in the Galilee region

## Archaeology

- The Samaria Ostraca — a collection of administrative pottery sherds from the 8th century BC recording deliveries of wine and oil — are widely associated with the administrative period of Jeroboam II's reign, reflecting the economic activity and prosperity Amos condemned
- Amos 6:4–6 references ivory couches and elaborate feasting, consistent with Assyrian-period ivory finds discovered at Samaria, suggesting real material wealth during this era
