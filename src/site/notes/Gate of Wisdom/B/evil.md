- Hebrew: ra'ah (רָעָה) — "evil, wickedness, calamity, distress, harm"; from the root r-'-a, ra'ah covers a wide range — moral evil (wrongdoing), and also disaster/calamity that befalls someone, making it a term that can describe both the wrong done and the harm suffered
- "God saw their works, that they turned from their evil (ra'ah) way. God relented of the disaster (ra'ah) which he said he would do to them, and he didn't do it" (Jonah 3:10, WEB)
- Greek (LXX): kakia/kakos (κακία / κακός) — "evil, wickedness, badness"; 

**Related words:**

- Ra (רַע) — the adjectival form, "evil, bad," used e.g. in Genesis 6:5 — "every imagination of the thoughts of his heart was only evil (ra) continually"
- Chata'ah (חַטָּאָה) / Chet (חֵטְא) — "sin, offense, missing the mark" — a related but distinct term for wrongdoing, more focused on transgression against a standard than ra'ah's broader "evil/harm" range
- Tov (טוֹב) — "good," the direct semantic opposite of ra, forming the foundational moral pair throughout the Tanakh, beginning at "the tree of the knowledge of good (tov) and evil (ra)" (Genesis 2:9, 17)

**Additional information:**

- This same dual-use of ra'ah (moral evil / resulting calamity) is a recurring wordplay throughout the Tanakh — the same term describes both the sin and its consequence, reflecting a Hebrew conceptual link between wrongdoing and the disaster it produces or invites
- Genesis 6:5 uses the adjectival form ra to describe the pre-Flood generation's total moral corruption, providing the closest thematic parallel to Nineveh's own described wickedness

**Connections:**

- Genesis 6:5 — "every imagination of the thoughts of his heart was only evil (ra) continually," part of the pre-Flood judgment narrative Jonah's own city-judgment account structurally echoes
- Jeremiah 18:8 — "if that nation turns from their evil (ra'ah), because of which I spoke against it, I will repent of the disaster (ra'ah) that I thought to do to them" (WEB) — the same double-use pattern found in Jonah 3:10, likely reflecting a shared prophetic idiom
