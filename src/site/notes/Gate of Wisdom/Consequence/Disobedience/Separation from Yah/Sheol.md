# Define

- Hebrew: sheol (שְׁאוֹל) — "the grave, the pit, the realm/abode of the dead"; in the Hebrew mindset, sheol is the common destination of all the dead — righteous and wicked alike — depicted as a place of darkness, silence, and the absence of activity, rather than a place of conscious reward or torment
- Greek: hades (ᾅδης) — the LXX's consistent rendering of sheol; carried into NT usage as the equivalent term for the realm of the dead

# Related Words

- Bor (בּוֹר) — "pit, cistern," used in poetic parallel with sheol (Psalm 30:3)
- Maveth (מָוֶת) — "death," frequently paired with sheol in parallelism (2 Samuel 22:6)
- Shachath (שַׁחַת) — "pit, corruption," another near-synonym paired with sheol (Isaiah 38:17-18)
- Hades (ᾅδης) — the Greek NT term inherited directly from the LXX's translation choice, e.g. Acts 2:27, 31; Revelation 1:18; 20:13-14

# Additional Information

- Sheol is consistently portrayed as a place with no activity, knowledge, or praise: "there is no work, nor device, nor knowledge, nor wisdom, in Sheol, where you are going" (Ecclesiastes 9:10, WEB); "the dead don't praise Yah, neither any who go down into silence" (Psalm 115:17, WEB)
- Sheol has no power to place itself beyond YHWH's reach or authority: "if I make my bed in Sheol, behold, you are there" (Psalm 139:8, WEB); "Sheol and Abaddon are before Yahweh; how much more then the hearts of the children of men!" (Proverbs 15:11, WEB)
- Sheol can be used both literally (descent of the body into the grave) and figuratively (a description of severe distress or near-death experience, as in Jonah's case, spoken from within the fish rather than from actual physical death)
- Sheol is depicted as a place from which YHWH has power to redeem: "I will ransom them from the power of Sheol. I will redeem them from death" (Hosea 13:14, WEB)

# Connections

- Jonah 2:2 — "From the belly of Sheol I cried" (WEB) — Jonah's figurative application of sheol-language to his situation inside the fish
- Psalm 18:4-6 — near-identical structure of "the cords of Sheol" surrounding the psalmist before a cry is heard by YHWH
- Matthew 12:40 — Yeshua's direct typological application of Jonah's sheol-like ordeal ("three days and three nights in the belly of the huge fish") to His own three days "in the heart of the earth"
- Acts 2:27, 31 — Peter's citation of Psalm 16:10 applied to Yeshua's resurrection, using hades, the same Greek term the LXX applies to sheol
- Revelation 1:18 — Yeshua's declared authority over death and hades: "I am the first and the last, and the Living one. I was dead, and behold, I am alive forevermore. Amen! And I have the keys of Death and of Hades" (WEB)
