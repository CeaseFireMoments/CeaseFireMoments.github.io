# Define
- Pentecost from the Greek word pentekoste ("fiftieth"), is the Greek name for [[Gate of Wisdom/Appointed Time/Shavuot\|Shavuot]] in the New Testament and Hellenistic Jewish texts (e.g., Philo and Josephus). It retains the same timing and agricultural significance.

# Additional Info

[[Zunpublished/Acts 2\|Acts 2]]:1-4 describes the pivotal New Testament event on Pentecost: the apostles, gathered in Jerusalem 50 days after Jesus’ resurrection (which coincided with [[Gate of Wisdom/Appointed Time/Passover\|Passover]]), to observe Shavuot. At that time, they experienced the descent of the Holy Spirit. They spoke in the tongues of the various nations (human languages), and Peter preached to a diverse crowd, leading to about 3,000 conversions ([[_The Scrolls/4. The Gospels and The Apostles/44 - Acts/Acts-02#v41\|Acts-02#v41]]).

The timing aligns with Shavuot, as Jews from various nations were in Jerusalem for the pilgrimage festival/[[Gate of Wisdom/Appointed Time/Feast\|Feast]] (Acts 2:5-11), fulfilling the biblical command to gather [[_The Scrolls/1. Torah (Law of Moses)/05 - Deuteronomy/Deut-16#v16\|Deut-16:16]]

In the New Testament, Pentecost marks the outpouring of the Holy Spirit. This event parallels the giving of the Torah on Sinai in Jewish tradition, as both involve God’s revelation to His people—law in the Old Testament, Spirit in the New Testament.

## Torah — Shavuot (Feast of Weeks)

- Exodus 19:1 — Israel arrives at Sinai in the third month, the same season Shavuot falls
- Exodus 19:16-19 — thunder, lightning, thick cloud, a very loud shofar blast, and the whole mountain quaking as YHWH descends in fire
- Exodus 19:18 — the mountain smoked because YHWH descended on it in fire
- Exodus 20:1-17 — the [[Gate of Wisdom/T/Ten Words\|Ten Words]] ([[Gate of Wisdom/C/Ten Commandments\|Ten Commandments]]) spoken directly by YHWH's voice to the people
- Exodus 20:18-19 — the people saw the thunderings, lightning, sound of the shofar, and the mountain smoking, and trembled, standing far off
- Exodus 24:3-8 — Moses reads the covenant to the people, they respond "all that YHWH has said we will do," and the covenant is sealed with blood
- Exodus 34:22 — the Feast of Weeks (Shavuot) named, tied to the firstfruits of wheat harvest. If the first fruits was accepted, then the whole harvest that followed would be accepted. 
- Leviticus 23:15-21 — count fifty days from the wave sheaf offering, then present two loaves of leavened bread baked with fine flour as firstfruits to YHWH, a holy convocation — [[Gate of Wisdom/Right Standing/Statutes (Chuqim)\|Statutes (Chuqim)]]
- Numbers 28:26-31 — additional burnt, sin, and peace [[Gate of Wisdom/Offering/Offerings\|Offerings]] prescribed for the day of [[Gate of Wisdom/Appointed Time/First Fruits\|First Fruits]]
- Deuteronomy 16:9-12 — count seven weeks from the sickle to the standing grain, keep the Feast of Weeks, rejoice before YHWH

## New Testament — Pentecost (Acts 2)

- Acts 2:1 — the disciples gathered together on the day of Pentecost
- Acts 2:2 — a sound from heaven like a rushing mighty wind filled the house
- Acts 2:3 — tongues like fire appeared, distributed, resting on each of them
- Acts 2:4 — all filled with the [[Gate of Wisdom/Ruach/Ruach\|Ruach]] HaKodesh, speaking in other [[Gate of Wisdom/T/tongues\|tongues]] as the Spirit gave them utterance
- Acts 2:5-11 — devout men from every nation under heaven hear the message each in their own language
- Acts 2:14-21 — Peter stands and declares this the fulfillment of [Joel 2:28-32](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Joel%202&version=WEB;NET;OJB), the outpouring of the Spirit "in the last days"
- Acts 2:37-41 — about three thousand souls added that day after Peter's message and their repentance and immersion

## Points of Contrast

- Sinai: fire descends on the mountain, the people stand at a distance, afraid (Exodus 20:18-19). 
	- Jerusalem: fire distributes and rests on each individual person (Acts 2:3)
- Sinai: one voice speaks, understood as one language by all present (Exodus 20:1). 
	- Jerusalem: many voices speak, heard by hearers in many differing languages (Acts 2:6, 2:8)
- Sinai: the covenant word is written on tablets of stone (Exodus 24:12, Exodus 31:18). 
	- Jeremiah 31:33 anticipates a day the law is written on the heart, which Pentecost's outpouring of the Spirit into believers directly enacts
- Sinai: the giving of Torah, the written word (Exodus 20:1-17). 
	- Jerusalem: the giving of the Ruach, empowering proclamation of the word (Acts 2:4, Acts 1:8)
- Sinai: fear and trembling, standing far off (Exodus 20:18). 
	- Jerusalem: boldness to speak and go near to the crowd (Acts 2:14, Acts 4:31)
- Both occur fifty days after a foundational deliverance event — Sinai fifty days after the Exodus/Passover (Exodus 19:1, counting from Exodus 12), 
	- Jerusalem fifty days after Yeshua's resurrection at Passover/Firstfruits (Leviticus 23:15-16, Acts 2:1)
- Both center on firstfruits: Shavuot's two loaves of leavened bread as firstfruits of harvest (Leviticus 23:17, 23:20); 
	- Acts 2 yields roughly three thousand as firstfruits of the gathered believers (Acts 2:41), echoed in James 1:18's language of "a kind of firstfruits"
- Sinai: those who violated the covenant that same day, worshiping the golden calf, were struck down — about three thousand fell (Exodus 32:28). 
	- Jerusalem: about three thousand were added to the number of souls through belief (Acts 2:41) — an inferred contrast in numbers, not a stated textual link**
